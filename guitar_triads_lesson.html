The Guitar Triad Masterclass

Triads can be played either way—all notes together as a chord (harmonically) or one note at a time in sequence (melodically/arpeggios). To master them without getting overwhelmed, you must isolate one string set at a time.

Part 1: The Core Practice Framework

  1. Start with One Key and One String Set: The top three strings (G, B, E) are highly audible and easy to fret. Pick a single major key—for example, G Major (G, B, D).
  2. Learn the 3 Inversions Vertically: Practice moving up the neck through Root Position (root at the bottom), First Inversion (3rd at the bottom), and Second Inversion (5th at the bottom).
  3. Use Core Routines:
    • The Inversion Ladder: Move up and down the neck through the shapes on a single string set.
    • The Cycle of Fourths/Fifths: Take one shape and move it to different keys around the neck.
    • Diatonic Scale Practice: Stay on one string set and play the chords of a scale sequentially up the fretboard.

Part 3: Smooth Voice Leading (G to C Changes)

Instead of jumping your entire hand across the neck, keep transitions small and efficient by finding the closest shapes in the same fretboard zone.

Top Strings (1, 2, 3)

Zone A (Frets 3 – 5):
• G Major (1st Inversion): 4 - 3 - 3
• C Major (2nd Inversion): 5 - 5 - 3
Movement: Index finger stays barred on the 3rd fret for both chords. Just shift other fingers to the 5th fret.
Zone B (Frets 7 – 9):
• G Major (2nd Inversion): 7 - 8 - 7
• C Major (Root Position): 9 - 8 - 8
Movement: Middle finger holds the 8th fret of the B string as a fixed anchor while other fingers swap.

Middle Strings (2, 3, 4)

Zone A (Frets 3 – 5):
• G Major (Root Position): 5 - 4 - 3
• C Major (2nd Inversion): 5 - 5 - 5
Movement: Keep ring finger on the 5th fret of the D string. Flatten it to a full 3-string barre for C.
Zone B (Frets 8 – 10):
• G Major (1st Inversion): 9 - 7 - 8
• C Major (Root Position): 10 - 9 - 8
Movement: Index finger stays anchored on the 8th fret of the B string. Other fingers shift up exactly one fret.
